How the site computes what it shows. Every number here is read from the code or from the index manifest at build time, so this text and the site cannot drift apart.

Data

The pool is every iNaturalist observation in British Columbia whose quality grade is still Needs ID. Each record contributes its first photo at iNaturalist's medium size. Records are added and removed as iNaturalist changes; the photo index is rebuilt in full now and then and grows by small daily updates between rebuilds (see Daily updates).

Model

Photos and species names are embedded by BioCLIP 2.5 Huge, a CLIP model with a ViT-H/14 image tower trained on the TreeOfLife-200M data set and later additions, __MODEL_ID__ at revision __MODEL_REVISION__ [1, 2]. Each photo is resized and centre-cropped to 224 pixels and becomes one vector of __DIMS__ numbers, scaled to unit length. Each scientific name in the pool becomes one text vector from the prompt "__PROMPT__"; the index holds __TAXA_COUNT__ of them. The reference for every score below is text only. No photo is ever the reference.

Scores

The match score of a photo to a species is the cosine between the photo vector and that species' text vector. For a record labelled at species, own is its score to the label and other is its best score to any other species in the pool; the margin is own minus other. A margin above 0 means the label is the model's first choice among the pool's species. For a record labelled above species (a genus, say), the best score inside the label's clade is compared with the best score outside it.

Identify levels

Every record is scored once and takes the first level whose rule it meets, in this order:

  1. Odd shots: the best score to any species is in the lowest __WEIRD_SHARE__ of the record's iconic group (birds, plants, fungi and so on). Any rank.
  2. Clear matches: species rank, margin above 0, own in the top __EASY_TOP_SHARE__ of the species' scored records, and the species has __MIN_PER_CLASS__ or more scored records.
  3. Close calls: species rank, margin below 0 but not below −__TAXONOMIST_MARGIN__, and the best other species has __MIN_LOOKALIKE_RECORDS__ or more verifiable records in BC.
  4. Narrow it down: a rank above species whose clade holds at least one species with a text vector. The score is the smaller of two margins: the in-clade best minus the best outside the clade, and the in-clade best minus the second-best in the clade.
  5. Might be mislabelled: species rank, margin below −__TAXONOMIST_MARGIN__, and the best other species has __MIN_LOOKALIKE_RECORDS__ or more verifiable records in BC.

The page shows Odd shots, Close calls and Might be mislabelled as one list. Within a level, records are ranked by their score and the rank is stored in __STEPS__ equal buckets; the bucket, not the raw score, orders the cards. Odd shots show the farthest first; Close calls and Might be mislabelled show the most negative margin first (see Evaluation for why). Levels order records for review; they do not say that a label is right or wrong.

Daily updates score only the records the last full run lacks, against cut-offs frozen from that run: the per-group Odd shots cut and, per species, the lowest own among its Clear matches. A species with fewer than __MIN_PER_CLASS__ records in the full run gets no Clear match until the next full run.

Confused pairs

Only records labelled at species count. A record is confused when its margin is below 0; it points to the species that beat its label. A species needs __MIN_PER_CLASS__ or more scored photos, a pair needs at least two photos pointing to the same other species, and each species lists at most __PAIRS_PER_SPECIES__ pairs with up to __RECORDS_PER_PAIR__ records each, the most negative margin first. A species' share is its confused photos divided by its scored photos.

Photo space

Closest matches are the __NEIGHBOR_K__ photos with the highest cosine to a photo, computed exactly against every photo in the index at the last full rebuild. The 2D layout is a t-SNE of the same vectors [3, 4], run with openTSNE as the manifest records it: __LAYOUT_DETAIL__. The layout is a lossy squeeze: on a random sample of __FID_N__ photos, the __FID_K__ nearest points on the plot keep on average __FID_MEAN__ of the __FID_K__ true closest matches (median __FID_MEDIAN__, and __FID_ZERO__ of sampled photos keep none). Nearness on the plot says two photos look alike to the model, not what either one is.

Text search and daily updates

Typed searches and daily updates use an approximate index of the same vectors: an inverted file of __NLIST__ lists with optimised product quantisation to __M__ codes of __NBITS__ bits each [5, 6, 7], scored by inner product, then a refine stage of __REFINE_BITS__ bits per code. The page probes __NPROBE__ lists and re-scores a shortlist of __SHORTLIST__ candidates.

A daily update embeds the new records, finds their closest matches among the new records exactly and in the index approximately (__UPDATE_NPROBE__ lists), and places each new photo on the plot at the cosine-weighted mean of its closest matches that already have a position. Existing records keep their closest matches and positions until the next full rebuild, so a new photo can list an old one as a match while the old one does not list it.

Host pairs

The look-alike statistics on the photo-space page skip pairs of families that are often photographed together, such as a host plant and its insect. Those pairs come from the Global Biotic Interactions database [8], release __GLOBI_RELEASE__, keeping family pairs with __GLOBI_MIN_ROWS__ or more recorded interactions. The confused-pairs table does not apply this filter.

Limits

The model sees one photo per record, so a record whose first photo is not the organism gets a poor score. Only names in the pool are candidates, so a species not yet observed in BC cannot be proposed. Text vectors carry what the model learned about a name during training, which is thin for rare or recently split taxa. Cut-offs were read from the distribution of scores in the BC pool and are not calibrated probabilities.

Evaluation

Do the levels predict what identifiers do? The species-labelled records scored on __EVAL_SCORED__ were looked up again in the pool of __EVAL_OUTCOME__. A record counts as corrected when it is still Needs ID under another taxon that still exists; a record whose old taxon vanished (a swap, merge or split) counts as neither. The rate ratio compares a level's corrected share with that of records in no level; the interval is a percentile bootstrap of __EVAL_REPS__ draws. Cohort: __EVAL_COHORT__ records. The script is scripts/levels_outcome.py and its output, with the input files' checksums, is committed beside this page's template.

Records in Might be mislabelled were corrected at several times the rate of records in no level, and Clear matches at a lower rate. The level order on the page follows these rates. Narrow it down is not tested: it has no species label to be corrected from. The window overlaps the page's launch, so some corrections may have been prompted by the page itself. The bars for this test were written down before the outcome was read.

Where did the corrected records go? Of the corrected records in Might be mislabelled, __SUG_MIS__ moved to the species the model suggested and __SUG_MIS_GENUS__ to that species' genus; for corrected records in no level the shares are __SUG_NONE__ and __SUG_NONE_GENUS__ (rate ratios __SUG_MIS_RR__ and __SUG_MIS_GENUS_RR__). The suggestion points in the right direction more often than chance, but it is rarely the exact answer, so the page shows it as a look-alike to check, not as a proposed ID. The bar set in advance for the exact species was a share of 0.25; it was not met. Records that left Needs ID are not counted here, since their new taxon is not in the pool.

Does the margin itself predict a correction? The next figure bins the same cohort into __CAL_BINS__ groups of equal size by margin, lowest first, and shows the corrected share in each. The share falls as the margin rises and is flat above 0. The cut-offs are not probabilities: a record in the lowest bin is corrected a few times in a thousand, not often.

Are the levels better than a plain sort? The area under the curve (AUC) is the chance that a corrected record is shown before an uncorrected one; 0.5 is a random order. The first column ranks every species-labelled record, the second only the records the Might be wrong list holds.

Within Close calls alone, showing the lowest margin first gives an AUC of __CLOSE_AUC__; the page used to show the margin nearest 0 first and was changed to this order on __CLOSE_CHANGED__. Across all records a plain sort by margin beats the page order, because the levels leave most records in no level and keep only __STEPS__ steps of order within a level; on the records the page shows, the two tie. The levels earn their place by what they say about a record, not by ranking it better than its margin would.
